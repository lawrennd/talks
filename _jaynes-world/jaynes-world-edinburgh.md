We suggest that Jaynes World could be a less teleological domain and one whose physicists are far more comfortable than ours.

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The Teleological Universe}

The world we live in is not a very satisfactory one from a philosophical perspective. Consider the constraints physicists tell us about:

* Objects seek to minimize their action (principle of least action)
* Photons obey a universal speed limit (but who enforces it?)
* Thermodynamic systems minimize their free energy

These principles suggest a teleological Universe - one with purpose or design built into its fundamental laws.

\subsection{An Alternative Inspired by Jaynes}

E.T. Jaynes developed powerful insights about our universe by maximising entropy. Inspired by this we consider a world where:

* time is not fundamental
* descending maximum entropy gradient is fundamental

\section{Maximising Entropy Production as the Global Invariant}

\subsection{Configuration Space and Entropy Gradients}

In Jaynes' World, we conceptualize the universe as a configuration space:

* This space represents all possible ways that $N$ bits of information can be configured in the universe
* There is no inherent time dimension in this space
* The universe "progresses" by descending the entropy gradient
* What we experience as the flow of time is actually movement along these entropy gradients.

\notes{The space is actually discrete, because a change in entropy will be a discrete reduction. But as $N$ gets very large, the space will well approximated as continuous.}

\subsection{The Origin as an Entropy Peak}

From any point in this configuration space:

* We can look up the entropy gradient to find a peak
* This peak is what we perceive as the "origin of the universe"
* Not all paths from this peak are valid - valid universes must follow maximum gradient trajectories
* Most configurations can only be reached by paths that aren't maximum gradient, making them invalid universes

\subsection{Characterizing the Origin}

The peak of the entropy gradient must have special properties:

* It must be the configuration with the largest potential to produce entropy
* It represents the state with maximum order from which disorder can increase
* This explains why our universe began in what appears to be a highly ordered state
* Wherever our universe actually begain, it would appear to have begun in a highly ordered state.

\notes{For a universe with $N$ bits of information, where the minimum space to store a bit is given by a volume of $v_0$, the length scale in each dimension is given by $\ell_i$ and there's a propagation constraint for information given by $c$ which allows information to travel a distance $d$ whenever we update the state of the universe.}
\notes{We look to place these $N$ bits of information in a configuration that minimises their entropy (and maximises their entropy potential). 

1. Each volume is connected with all other particles, meaning they are all in informational contact with each other and live in a $D=N-1$ dimensional space.
2. The total volume, $V=N\prod_i^{D} \ell_i$ is just large enough to contain the bits, $N=V/v_0$. 
3. We impose conformal symmetry on the space to futher reduce the entropy.}

\subsection{Least Action as a Consequence}

In Jaynes' World we perceive time because:

* Processes in at appropriate scales and reference frames form the same rhythm as drop down the potential.
* Think of this as a metronome effect.
  * This appears to observers as a "principle of least action".

\subsection{When Time Goes Funny}

* In regions of high entropy gradient "principle of least action" gives a slower moving metronome.
* At low energy levels and scales entropy production involves multiple paths (like Feynman interpretation of Quantum mechanics). 
  * In these cases there is no single threaded time. 

\section{Emergence of Physical Laws}

\subsection{Laws as Emergent Properties}

In Jaynes' World:

* Physical laws aren't fundamental but emerge from entropy gradients
* Particles are the quantisation of information in the "information topography".
* To observers entropy flows appear as "physical laws".


\section{Implications}

\subsection{A Less Teleological Universe}

In Jaynes' World:

* Physical laws aren't about purpose or design
* They're about information and entropy
* The universe isn't telling particles what to do - it's simply following the maximum entropy path

\subsection{More Comfortable Physics}

Physicists in Jaynes' World would be more comfortable because:

* No need to explain why particles "know" to minimize action
* No need for universal traffic police enforcing the speed of light
* A clearer understanding of why thermodynamic systems behave as they do

\section{Conclusion}

Jaynes' world is just gradient descent in a configuration space but:

* Observers in the world see time as an emergent phenomenon. 
* They see the origin of their universe as a highly ordered state which rapidly expands.
* The end of their universe is in a minimum of entropy potential.
  * To observers it looks like time is infinite, but in reality there is only finite entropy production. 
* We see universes are only valid if they sit on a descent trajectory from the origin point.

\notes{Jaynes' World offers us a perspective where physics makes more philosophical sense, even if the mathematics remains unchanged - a universe governed not by mysterious teleological principles but by the fundamental tendency of entropy to increase along specific paths.}
