**DESCRIPTION OF CONTENTS OF THE COURSE**

*This masterclass will mainly focus on the strategic and organisational
challenges that AI creates for business leaders, aiming to provide
students with the tools and knowledge to lead organisations through the
AI transformation. The course covers four main areas: (1) understanding
the fundamental differences between human and machine intelligence
through the lens of embodiment factors and information topography; (2)
how AI reshapes organisational decision-making structures, including the
balance between centralised control and devolved authority; (3)
maintaining human judgment and building trust in AI systems, examined
through real-world governance failures such as the UK Post Office
Horizon scandal; and (4) strategic AI implementation frameworks focused
on the attention economy, human capital, and people-first approaches.
Throughout, students engage with conceptual frameworks drawn from Neil
Lawrence's book The Atomic Human, supported by case studies, group
exercises, and interactive discussion.*

**INTENDED LEARNING OUTCOMES (ILOs)**

Upon completion of this masterclass, students will be able to: (1)
explain the key differences between human and machine intelligence using
the concept of embodiment factors; (2) analyse how AI changes the
information topography of organisations and the implications for
decision-making architecture; (3) apply strategic frameworks for AI
implementation that preserve human judgment and agency; (4) evaluate
real-world cases of algorithmic governance failure and identify
appropriate safeguards; (5) design human-machine collaboration models
for their own organisational context; and (6) develop board-level
literacy around AI governance and risk.

**TEACHING METHOD**

Please find below the rationale for the teaching methods, explaining how
the course design meets the School policies and how specific assessment
methods align with the learning goals stated.

*Please find a list of suggested options. The list is non-exclusive:
Direct Instruction, Flipped Classroom, Kinaesthetic Learning,
Inquiry-Based Learning, Expeditionary Learning, Case Studies, etc.*

The masterclass employs a blended approach combining Direct Instruction,
Case Studies, and Inquiry-Based Learning. Four 45-minute lectures
alternate with four structured group exercises, ensuring immediate
application of conceptual frameworks to real organisational challenges.
This design reflects School policies on active learning and
assessment-for-learning: lectures provide rigorous conceptual grounding
(e.g. embodiment factors, information topography, the attention
economy), while exercises require students to map information flows,
conduct SWOT analyses, analyse a governance failure case (UK Post Office
Horizon scandal), and develop an institutional AI strategy. The plenary
debrief after each exercise provides formative feedback and exposes
students to diverse institutional perspectives (start-up, government,
established player, SME).
